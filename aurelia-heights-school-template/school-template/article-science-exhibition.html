Student Science Exhibition Draws Industry Judges

Twenty-two student research projects were showcased to a panel of visiting engineers and scientists at this year's Aurelia Heights science exhibition, held in the main hall on July 12th. Projects ranged from a Primary 6 water-filtration model to an A-Level investigation into solar panel efficiency under variable cloud cover — the latter conducted using equipment built partly in-house in the school's design-technology workshop.

The exhibition is judged each year by a rotating panel drawn from local engineering firms and Makerere University's science faculty, giving students feedback from people working in the fields they're studying rather than only from their own teachers. This year's panel included two Aurelia Heights alumni now working as practicing engineers in Kampala.

What stood out to the judges

Judges singled out the depth of data collection across several projects — multiple entries included several weeks of logged measurements rather than a single demonstration — and the quality of student explanations under questioning. "It wasn't just a poster with results on it," said one judge. "Every student I spoke to could tell me exactly why their method worked, and what they'd have done differently with more time."

The overall exhibition prize went to a Senior 5 team's project on low-cost water testing kits for rural clinics, which the judging panel noted had a genuinely practical application beyond the classroom.

What's next

Several exhibition projects will be entered into this year's Regional Science Fair in October, where Aurelia Heights has placed in the top three for three consecutive years. Preparation sessions for interested students begin in the first week of the new term.
