About Aurelia Heights

Our Story

A learning community built on excellence, character and possibility — and the people and decisions that got us here.

How We Started

A School Built Slowly, On Purpose.

Aurelia Heights opened in 1998 with two classrooms and eleven students, started by a group of Kampala educators who felt the city needed a school willing to take character development as seriously as exam results. Growth was deliberate rather than rapid — a new year group was added only once the previous one was working well, not on a fixed schedule.

Today the school serves roughly 1,500 students from Early Years through A-Level, but the founding instinct hasn't changed: expand capacity only as fast as we can maintain the attention every student gets here. Many of our current teachers were taught by the teachers who came before them — a continuity that shows up in how the school is actually run, not just in its marketing.

What We Stand For

Vision

A Kampala where every student who passes through Aurelia Heights leaves better equipped — academically and personally — than the systems around them expected.

Mission

To combine rigorous academics with genuine character development, delivered by teachers who know each student well enough for that combination to actually work.

Our Approach to Learning

Rigor and character aren't on separate timetables here.

"

We don't treat academic performance and personal character as a trade-off — a student who's learned to prepare properly, listen carefully, and take a genuine interest in getting something right is usually also the student doing well in class.

That belief shapes practical decisions: class sizes small enough for teachers to notice a struggling student early, a reading culture that isn't optional, and leadership responsibilities given to real students rather than reserved for a prize-day speech. Critical thinking gets built through debate, lab work and project-based assignments rather than memorization alone — and community involvement is treated as part of a student's education, not an extracurricular add-on.
